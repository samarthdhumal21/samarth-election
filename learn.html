Election Process Journey

Step-by-step guide to how Indian elections work.

Stage 1: Announcement of Elections

The Election Commission of India (ECI) announces the election schedule. This immediately enforces the Model Code of Conduct (MCC).

Key Fact: The MCC prevents ruling parties from using government resources for campaigning.
The announcement is made via a press conference. The ECI details dates for nominations, polling, and counting. From this exact moment, new government schemes or projects cannot be announced.

Stage 2: Voter Registration

Updating the electoral rolls to include new voters (18+) and remove deceased or shifted voters.

Key Fact: You must be a citizen of India and 18 years old on the qualifying date to vote.
Booth Level Officers (BLOs) visit homes to verify voters. Citizens can register online via Form 6. A final electoral roll is published before the elections.

Stage 3: Filing of Nominations

Candidates submit their nomination papers and affidavits detailing their assets and criminal records.

Key Fact: Candidates must deposit a security amount, which is forfeited if they get less than 1/6th of total valid votes.
Nominations are submitted to the Returning Officer (RO). Affidavits are made public so voters know the background of the candidates.

Stage 4: Scrutiny & Withdrawal

The RO checks nominations for validity. Valid candidates get a few days to withdraw if they change their minds.

Key Fact: If a candidate's affidavit is found to have false information, their nomination can be rejected.
After the withdrawal period ends, the final list of contesting candidates is published and symbols are allotted to independent candidates.

Stage 5: Election Campaign

Candidates hold rallies and distribute manifestos. Campaigning stops 48 hours before voting begins.

Key Fact: Lok Sabha candidates have an expenditure limit of ₹95 Lakhs in larger states.
The last 48 hours is known as the "Silent Period". No public meetings, processions, or loudspeakers are allowed to give voters a peaceful time to think.

Stage 6: Voting Day

Voters cast their ballot using EVMs and VVPATs at designated polling stations under tight security.

Key Fact: VVPAT allows voters to see a printed slip for 7 seconds verifying their vote was cast correctly.
Indelible ink is applied to the left index finger to prevent duplicate voting. The Presiding Officer oversees the booth.

Stage 7: Vote Counting

EVMs are brought to counting centers. Votes are tallied under the supervision of Observers and candidates' agents.

Key Fact: EVMs do not connect to the internet, making remote hacking impossible.
Counting is done in rounds. A mandatory matching of EVM counts with VVPAT slips from 5 randomly selected booths per constituency is done.

Stage 8: Result Declaration

The RO declares the winner who receives a Certificate of Election. Later, elected members take their oath.

Key Fact: A candidate wins if they get the most votes, even if it's not a majority (First-Past-The-Post system).
The ECI submits the list of elected members to the President/Governor. This concludes the electoral process for that constituency.
