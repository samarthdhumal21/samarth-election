SECTION I — HOW AN ELECTRONIC VOTING MACHINE (EVM) WORKS

The Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) constitutes the primary instrument of democratic franchise in the Republic of India. Mandated by the Election Commission of India (ECI) pursuant to Section 61A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the EVM is an electronically sealed, standalone, tamper-evident apparatus.

BALLOT UNIT

  • Placed inside the screened voting compartment (voter privacy).
  • Displays candidates' names, party symbols & serial numbers.
  • Blue illuminated button assigned to each candidate.
  • Pressing button records vote & triggers audible beep.
  • Connected to Control Unit via a 5-metre cable.

CONTROL UNIT

  • Retained exclusively by the Presiding Officer at polling desk.
  • Features: Ballot, Result, Close, Print & Clear buttons.
  • Stores all votes in non-volatile memory (no internet).
  • Powered by a 6V alkaline battery — no electricity needed.

VOTER VERIFIABLE PAPER AUDIT TRAIL (VVPAT)

  • An independent verification system attached to the EVM.
  • Upon casting a vote, VVPAT prints a slip displaying the candidate's serial number, name, and party symbol.
  • Slip is visible through a transparent glass window for 7 seconds.

SECTION II — THE ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA (ECI)

The Election Commission of India is a permanent, independent, and autonomous constitutional authority established under Article 324 of the Constitution of India. It is vested with the superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections.

Date of Establishment

25th January 1950 (National Voters' Day)

Constitutional Provision

Article 324 of the Constitution of India

Composition

1 Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) + 2 Election Commissioners

Jurisdiction

Parliament, State Legislatures, President & Vice-President elections

Headquarters

Nirvachan Sadan, Ashoka Road, New Delhi — 110 001

Key Legislation

Representation of the People Act, 1950 & 1951

SECTION III — ELECTORAL PROCESS TIMELINE AT A GLANCE

The electoral process in India follows a meticulously sequenced schedule promulgated by the Election Commission of India. Each phase is regulated by the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

DAY 1: Press Conference & MCC Enforcement

The ECI convenes a press conference to announce the official poll schedule. The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) enters immediate legal force.

WK 2–4: Gazette Notification, Nomination & Scrutiny

Candidates file nomination papers before the Returning Officer. Scrutiny follows; defective or fraudulent nominations are summarily rejected.

WK 4–8: Campaigning Phase

Political parties and candidates campaign under ECI supervision. Expenditure limits apply. Campaigning ceases 48 hours prior to polling ('Silent Period').

POLL DAY: Electronic Voting — Polling Day

Polling stations operate 07:00–18:00 hrs. Voters cast ballots via EVM-VVPAT. Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF) deployed.

RESULT: Vote Counting & Declaration of Results

Counting commences at the designated Counting Centre. EVM results tallied round-by-round. The winning candidate receives a Certificate of Election.
